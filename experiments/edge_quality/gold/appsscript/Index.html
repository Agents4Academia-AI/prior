Category definitions

Classify why the citing paper cites the one work marked [THIS CITATION]. Other citations in the passage are context only — do not classify those. Judge from the passage and the cited abstract, never from your own knowledge of the papers.

background

The target is cited as general context, prior art, motivation, or a passing mention. The citing work neither builds on it nor singles it out for critique.

Includes grouped critique: if the target is lumped with other papers to describe a general limitation of the field, it is still background.

“current systems [THIS CITATION] struggle with X” · “while these studies show Y, they lack Z” · “unlike these prior works…”

uses_extends

The citing work explicitly uses, adopts, or builds on the target. The text must show active first-person adoption. Merely describing the target's mechanics in detail is not enough.

“we use the dataset from [THIS CITATION]” · “following the framework of [THIS CITATION]” · “we reuse their evaluation protocol”

compares_contrasts

The citing work sets its own approach or results against the target specifically: distinguishes itself from the target alone, critiques a limitation of the target, or reports a differing result.

“unlike [THIS CITATION]” · “our work differs from [THIS CITATION]” · “we outperform [THIS CITATION]”

Tie-breakers

The grouping rule. If the contrastive cue (“However…”, “In contrast…”) applies to a whole group or to “the current state of the art”, choose background. The contrast must single out the target itself.
Baselines. Target cited as a competing method to beat or compare results against → compares_contrasts. Target cited because you adopted its protocol, dataset or code to run those tests → uses_extends.
background vs compares_contrasts. Genuinely unsure → background, unless there is an explicit contrastive cue tied to the target alone.
background vs uses_extends. Ask whether the citing authors' own method relies on that work to function. If yes → uses_extends.
Multiple intents in one passage? Pick the one that dominates at this citation. Too garbled or generic to tell → background, and say so in the notes.
